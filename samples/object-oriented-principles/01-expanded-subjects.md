The SWEBOK Guide’s discussion of object‐oriented design (OOD) is embedded within a broader treatment of software design principles and strategies. Based on the content, here are five closely related topics that provide deeper insights into object‐oriented principles:

* Core Object‐Oriented Concepts
  Fundamental concepts such as abstraction, encapsulation, inheritance, and polymorphism form the backbone of object‐oriented programming. These principles are critical for defining how objects represent both data and behavior in software systems .

* Responsibility-Driven Design
  As an alternative underlying principle of OOD, responsibility-driven design focuses on assigning clear responsibilities to objects, ensuring that each object plays a specific role in the system. This approach complements the traditional focus on data abstraction .

* SOLID Principles
  The guide highlights design strategies using mnemonics like SOLID (Single-responsibility, Open–closed, Liskov substitution, Interface segregation, and Dependency inversion). These principles guide class design and help maintain modularity and flexibility in object-oriented systems .

* Component-Based Design (CBD)
  Evolving from object-oriented design, CBD emphasizes the development and reuse of standalone components with well-defined interfaces. This approach further encapsulates object-oriented principles in a scalable and reusable manner .

* Object-Oriented Analysis and Design Methods
  Beyond design techniques, the guide discusses methods that leverage object-oriented models—using diagrams and progressive refinement—to capture the system’s structure and behavior. These methods support the transition from analysis to detailed design and ultimately to implementation .

These topics together not only cover the core theoretical aspects of object-oriented principles but also illustrate practical strategies and methods for implementing and extending them in modern software engineering.
